## Introduction {#introduction}

Blind Skills is a directory of 84 free videos and podcasts that each teach a blindness skill: a technique for doing a task without sight. Skills here include cane travel, guiding, braille, cooking, dressing, labeling, handling money, and crafts.

A lesson is included when all of the following are true:

- It teaches a technique a blind or low vision person can use to do a task nonvisually, or it teaches a guide or teacher how to support that technique. A talk about choices or experiences, however helpful, is not a lesson.
- The technique applies generally. A lesson on one feature of one app or device belongs in [Tech Training][p-techtraining] instead.
- It is free, needs no sign-in, and plays at once: its address is either an audio file or a video page that the free mpv player, with yt-dlp, plays directly.

**Structured discovery** is the way the training centers of the National Federation of the Blind, and agencies that follow them, teach these skills. Instead of giving step-by-step answers, the instructor asks questions that lead the student to gather information nonvisually and solve the problem. Students with some vision often wear sleepshades so they learn to trust nonvisual techniques. A lesson's Structured discovery field says Subject when the lesson is about the method, and Setting when a center or agency that teaches this way made it.

Lessons are grouped by kind of skill, under a heading at level 2 for each group, with a one-sentence definition. Each lesson title is a heading at level 3 and a link straight to the audio file or video. Under it come fields in alphabetical order, shown only when a real value is known: Audience, Description, Duration, Episode page or Source page, Medium, Presenter, Series, Skill, Source, Structured discovery, Transcript, and Year. Skill names the task the lesson teaches. A year appears only when the publisher states one. Links were found on publishers' own pages or in search results in October 2026; playing every lesson through was not tested, and some video hosts ask a browser to press Play first.

**The whole directory is also a play list.** [BlindSkills.m3u][p-m3u] lists every lesson in the order of this page, with its title. Open it in FileDir's Homer Player (Control+Shift+H) or Play List (Control+Shift+L), or in any player that reads M3U files, to hear the lessons one after another. In FileDir, the facts under each heading here, such as Description and Duration, travel with the track when the page itself is opened as a play list.

The directory also comes as BlindSkills, a database included with [DbDo][p-dbdo], a free database program designed for screen reader users. There, Alt+Shift+P plays a lesson, and marked lessons play as a list.

Statistics come next, then a note on the major organizations of and for the blind. Five appendixes list the lessons again by source, by year, by title, by medium, and by skill, each linking back to the full entry.

Each lesson belongs to its publisher. This directory's own text and arrangement are available under [CC BY-SA 4.0][cc-by-sa]. It is English-language in scope. Companion directories are [Tech Training][p-techtraining] and [Blind Movers][p-movers].

## By the Numbers {#by-the-numbers}

- 84 lessons in 12 groups; the largest group is [Orientation and Mobility](#cat-orientation-and-mobility), with 20 (24%).
- 69 videos and 15 audio lessons; every audio lesson is a podcast episode that plays as an MP3 file.
- The 84 lessons teach 68 distinct skills. The most taught is the human guide technique, with 8 lessons from British, Canadian, and American publishers; white cane technique follows, with 13.
- Schools for the blind made 32 lessons, more than twice as many as the national organizations of and for the blind, which made 14 together. The largest single source is Washington State School for the Blind, with 31 short skill clips.
- Where a length is known, lessons run from 1:44 ([Trailing Technique](#skill-trailing-technique)) to about 121 minutes ([Holiday Braille Drawings](#skill-holiday-braille-drawings)): a short video shows one technique, while a recorded class teaches a whole project.
- 3 lessons are marked as structured discovery: [Cane Travel](#skill-cane-travel) (Setting), [Maureen Teaching Warren at the Colorado Center for the Blind](#skill-maureen-teaching-warren-at-the-colorado-center-for-the-blind) (Setting), [Using Principles of Structured Discovery When Learning Technology](#skill-using-principles-of-structured-discovery-when-learning-technology) (Subject).
- 8 lessons link a transcript, 9 give their duration, and 18 have a year the publisher states.

## Major Organizations {#major-organizations}

These organizations were searched on purpose. Many of their recordings are meetings, news, or advocacy rather than lessons, so those are not listed.

- **American Council of the Blind:** five craft classes from ACB Crafters, taught by ear, on knitting, braiding, and braille drawing.
- **American Foundation for the Blind:** two short videos on writing braille, by slate and by braillewriter.
- **CNIB:** two videos on the sighted guide technique.
- **National Federation of the Blind:** its seminar on structured discovery, plus a lesson filmed at the Colorado Center for the Blind, one of its three training centers. The centers post few lesson recordings; most of what they publish describes their programs.
- **RNIB:** three videos on guiding.
- **Vision Support America:** one lesson, on trailing a wall; its other videos are mostly tips for using remaining vision. It is a free initiative of Braille Institute.
- **World Blind Union:** no lesson found; its recordings are mainly advocacy and policy.
